A portfolio is a set of pictures that shows how you look when someone else is directing you. It is not a highlight reel of every outfit you own, and it is not a promise that an agency will sign you. Agencies differ. Ask the one you care about what they want to see, then we build toward that, not toward a rumour.

I photograph model portfolios in and around Edmonton because this city already looks like work: glass, winter roads, bridges, snow, and rooms with plain walls. You do not need a flight to have range. You need a plan, and you need pictures that tell the truth about your proportions, your face, and how you take direction.

The fashion end of what I do lives on the [fashion and editorial](/fashion-editorial/) pages and on the [runway](/runway/) page. A portfolio session is the quieter, more useful cousin of that work. On the services list it is simple: fresh images for agencies, comp cards, and your book.

## What a portfolio is for

A book has a job. Someone who does not know you should be able to see your height in a frame, your face without a distraction, and at least one picture where you are in motion or in a real environment. If every frame is a close beauty shot, they still do not know how you stand. If every frame is a tiny figure in a landscape, they still do not know your face.

I am not your agent, and I will not write the pitch. I will tell you, in the session, when a pose is selling a shape and when it is hiding one. Hiding is sometimes fashion. Hiding in every frame is how a book becomes vague.

Bring the truth about your experience. If you have never been on a set, say so. Most people have never been directed before, and that is fine. I would rather start there than discover it after we have burned the best light on poses you cannot feel yet.

If you already have images, send the ones you still like and the ones that got you meetings, if any. I will not copy another photographer's set. I will look for the hole: no full length, no daylight, no simple face, no outerwear, no proof you can walk. We fill the hole.

## Full length, beauty, and movement

I plan three kinds of frame, and I tell you which one we are making so you can stop performing the other two.

Full length is information. Feet in the frame when the shoe matters, headroom that does not crop your posture, clothes that show a waist or honestly refuse to. A jacket and trousers against a plain wall can do more for a book than a gown that hides your legs. I still love a gown. It just should not be the only sentence.

Beauty is the face, close enough that skin, bone, and expression are the subject. Hard midday sun is a choice. Soft window light is a choice. I will not smooth you into a person you are not, and I will not promise a retouch that invents a different face. If you want a beauty look with a makeup artist, bring one or ask me for a local recommendation. I do not do the makeup.

Movement is the picture where a still pose would lie. A walk, a turn, a coat in wind, a shoulder that arrives a second before the rest of you. Edmonton wind will volunteer for this. I use it on bridges and open paths, and I avoid it when we need a still beauty frame. The [location note](/journal/edmonton-photoshoot-locations/) is where I describe those paths. Public places stay public. We do not block them, and anything bigger than a small session on foot needs a check of the City of Edmonton rules. I will not invent those rules in a journal piece.

## Edmonton as a working city

Alberta gives you seasons that coastal cities do not shoot the same way. Snow is not a novelty here. A winter full-length on a road with bare trees can look severe and expensive if the clothes are simple and you are warm enough to keep your face kind. Summer balconies and glass give you skyline without a studio rental. A leather jacket in a white wall of snow is a fashion picture this city can actually deliver.

I will not pretend every agency in Toronto or Vancouver is waiting on an Edmonton skyline. Some markets want a clean studio digi more than they want a mood. If that is what you were asked for, we can do plain, well-lit, full-length frames with a neutral background when we have that setup. Say the words "they asked for clean." I will stop making poems and start making information.

Range still matters inside a local book. One indoor or shaded beauty, one full length, one environmental frame, one look that shows how you wear a commercial outfit rather than only a costume. Four honest pictures beat twenty that repeat a single idea. Show coverage is a different job from a tested portrait, so a portfolio day stays a portfolio day. The [runway](/runway/) page is the event work.

## Comp cards and the edit

A comp card is a tight selection, not the whole gallery. I deliver a private gallery, and then we choose. For a card, I look for a face, a length, and a picture with a little life, plus whatever specific hole your agency named. I will not pad a card with near-duplicates to make it look longer.

Cropping is part of the edit. A full-length frame that only works if I cut you at the knee was the wrong frame, and I would rather have shot the right one. That is why I leave time for the length, even when the close frames are the ones you feel most comfortable in.

Black and white is a tool, not a fix. If a colour frame is weak, removing the colour will not give it a pose. I use black and white when the shape and the light are already strong and the colour is a distraction.

I do not invent tear sheets, magazine credits, or client names you do not have. If a picture is from a personal session, it is a personal session. Claiming otherwise is how books get distrusted, and I will not help with that.

## How a session with me runs

We talk first. You tell me who the book is for, what they asked to see, and what you can actually wear and walk in. I suggest looks, locations, and timing. We meet on location or in a studio setup, and I direct the session. The edited images arrive in a private gallery, usually within two weeks. Those are the same steps as the rest of my [services](/services/), with the edit pointed at a book instead of a family frame.

Two or three looks are enough for a focused portfolio day if they are different from each other. A gown, a simple commercial outfit, and a coat will cover more ground than five dresses of the same length. Bring pieces that fit when you lift your arms. The [what to wear](/journal/what-to-wear-photoshoot/) note covers fit. Tell me about injuries, heels you cannot do, or cold you cannot stay in, and I will change the order.

## What I will not invent for you

I will not promise representation. I will not quote an agency's rules from memory and call them current. I will not put a price in this article, because a two-look test and a full day are different, and pricing is on request. I will not put your face on a campaign you did not do.

What I will do is direct you clearly, shoot the frames a book actually needs, and give you an edit you can stand behind. If that is the session you want, [book it](/book/) and write "model portfolio" as the type, plus who asked you for pictures and what they named. I will reply with a plan and with options. A book can be beautiful. It still has to tell the truth at a glance.
