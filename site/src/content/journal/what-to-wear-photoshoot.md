People freeze when I ask what they want to wear. They think the pictures require a costume, or a new wardrobe, or a colour that photographs "correctly." Most of the time they need one outfit they can move in, and a second option if the weather turns. I direct the session. I am not here to make you feel late to your own clothes.

Fashion is the work I love most, and the [editorial pictures](/fashion-editorial/) show how far a look can go. A portrait or a couple's session can be quieter than that and still be sharp. The rule I actually use is the same in both cases: the clothes should survive the location, and you should still look like yourself when we are done.

## Start with how you want to feel

Before the hanger, I ask for a feeling. Polished, easy, cold and graphic, soft, severe, a little glam. Those words pick clothes faster than a Pinterest board of strangers. If you bring me three outfits that all say different things, we will spend the first twenty minutes negotiating, and the light will not wait.

If you are stuck, send me two pictures of yourself you already like, even if they are old. I can see the neckline, the colour, and the amount of skin you are comfortable with. I will not push a look that makes you tug at the fabric the whole time. Nerves are normal. A dress you have to manage is not.

Bring the thing you already wear when you feel most like the person you want the picture to remember. Then we edit. A portrait for your brand, a profile picture, or a polished personal frame all live under [portraits](/services/) on the services page, and the clothes should match the use. A boardroom portrait and a river-valley editorial do not share a shoe.

## Colour against an Edmonton backdrop

This city has a lot of grey stone, dark steel, snow, and a blue sky that can be almost rude. I plan colour against that, not against a white studio sweep, unless we are actually in a studio.

Black is reliable here. It holds a shape against snow and against the Legislature sky. Red is a decision. A red gown in snow, or a red sweater in autumn trees, will be the subject, and everything else will support it. I love that when we mean it. I avoid it when you wanted a quiet face and the coat is shouting.

White and very pale colours need care in snow. They can merge with the ground. A dark coat, a boot, a belt, or a shadow from the building gives the eye a place to land. Cream and ivory do this more kindly than pure white, which is also why bridal notes live in their own piece: [bridal portraits](/journal/bridal-portraits-edmonton/).

Patterns are welcome when they are large enough to read. A tiny print turns to noise once I step back for a full-length frame. Solids, leather, wool, and one strong colour usually beat a busy head-to-toe look.

Edmonton also has real winter, and the pretty coat is sometimes the coat that keeps you out for ten extra minutes. I will choose the warm one if the thin one makes you miserable. Miserable reads on a face faster than any stylist wants to admit.

## Texture beats a perfect match

I would rather have texture than a matched set. Lace, a knit, a leather sleeve, the sheen of a gown, the matte of wool: those catch light differently, and the picture gets depth without a complicated pose. A head-to-toe outfit from one shop can look like a mannequin. One good piece, worn with something plain you already own, usually looks like a person.

Shine is a tool. Sequins and satin throw hot highlights if the sun is hard. I can use that on a fashion frame. On a beauty portrait I often want the light on your face, not on a shoulder that is louder than you are. Tell me if you love the sparkle. I will put it where it behaves.

Fit matters more than the label. If a jacket pulls at the button when you lift an arm, I will see it in every frame where you move. Sit in the clothes at home. Raise your arms. Walk. If you cannot do those things, the session will be a series of stiff stands, and that is not how I like to work.

## Shoes, layers, and the walk

Locations in this city involve walking. The valley has grades. Bridge paths are long. Snow hides ice. I wrote about those places in [where I like to shoot](/journal/edmonton-photoshoot-locations/), and the shoe advice is the practical half of that note.

Bring the shoe you want in the picture, and a shoe you can walk in. We change at the spot, on flat ground. I will not ask you to hike a heel down a river stair for the sake of a frame. If the picture is a full-length fashion pose, the heel can still be the picture. It just should not be the transportation.

Layers let us adjust without a car trip. A coat for the walk, off for the frame, back on between setups. Scarves are useful until they cover the neckline we wanted. Hats are useful until the shadow eats your eyes. I will say which one is helping.

If we are shooting more than one look, two is plenty for a portrait session. A fashion or model day can hold more, and that is a different booking. Stuffing six outfits into an hour makes every picture average.

## Hair, makeup, and comfort

You can arrive ready, or you can bring your own hair and makeup artist. I am glad when you do. I can also recommend local artists if you want a name. I do not do the makeup myself, and I will not pretend a five-minute powder in a parking lot replaces someone who knows your face.

If you do your own, keep it in the family of how you actually like to look, then push one thing: a stronger lip, a cleaner brow, a little more definition at the eye. Outdoor light is not a ring light. Very pale makeup can vanish. Very heavy makeup can look like a mask in daylight. The middle is usually where portraits live.

Wind will undo a style that cannot move. Tell your artist if we are on a bridge. A look that needs a wind-free room will fight Walterdale, and the fight will be in the pictures.

Eat something. Cold people and hungry people get pale and short-tempered, and I would rather spend the session directing you than reviving you. Water in the bag. A lip colour you can reapply. Tissues. The unglamorous kit is part of the craft.

## A simple packing list

One outfit you love, fitted and walked-in at home. One backup if the sky changes or a spill happens. The picture shoe and the walking shoe. A layer for the temperature we actually have, not the temperature we hoped for. A bag that closes. A clip or tie if your hair needs a break from your face.

Skip the brand-new stiff jeans, the tag you forgot to cut, and the piece you bought only because a stranger online wore it in a different climate. If you are unsure, send me photos of the options on your body in daylight, by a window. I will pick, or I will ask you to lose one of them.

Pricing for the session is on request, because the clothes are not the quote. The time, the place, and the use of the pictures are. When you are ready, [book a shoot](/book/) and tell me the feeling in one sentence. I will answer with locations and a plan, not with a lecture about trends.

If the session is for two, read the [season by season couples note](/journal/engagement-photos-edmonton/) as well. Matching outfits are optional. Looking like you went on purpose is not.
