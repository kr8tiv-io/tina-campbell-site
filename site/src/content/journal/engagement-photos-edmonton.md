A couple's session should look like the two of you on a day you actually had, directed just enough that the hands know where to go. I am not interested in a pile of tricks you will never repeat. I am interested in the way you stand when you forget the camera, and in a few frames where you remember it and still look steady.

Edmonton makes that easy and hard in the same year. The light, the ground, and the amount of coat you need change every few months. I plan the session for the season we are in, not for a mood board from another climate. If you want the fashion end of my work, it is on the [editorial pages](/fashion-editorial/). Couples sit quieter than that, and the [services](/services/) line is simple: easy, styled sessions for two.

## Spring, when the valley wakes up

Spring in Edmonton is a rumour and then a week of melt. The river valley goes brown before it goes green. That brown can be beautiful if we treat it as texture, and ugly if you wanted blossoms I cannot promise. I will not sell you cherry trees. I will sell you long paths, new light, and the first days you can stand outside without planning your exit.

Dress for mud. The picture coat can be light. The shoe you walk in should not be the shoe you mourn. We change on pavement. If the path is a slick of melt, I move us. A twisted ankle is not a memory I want attached to your pictures.

Spring sessions are good for people who want colour in their clothes, because the landscape has not taken all the colour yet. A pale blue dress, a dark jacket, a simple knit: they read clearly against a hillside that is still waking up. I wrote more about colour in [what to wear](/journal/what-to-wear-photoshoot/).

## Summer light that stays late

Summer is the generous season, and it makes people casual about time. The sun stays up. The river paths fill with everyone who had the same idea. I book a start that sounds early to you and reasonable to me, because the last hour is shared with cyclists, weddings, and dogs who have never seen a camera they did not want to greet.

Heat is the other constraint. A black outfit looks sharp and feels like a decision you regret at minute thirty. I build in shade. We shoot the sun as a rim on the edges of you, then we step under trees for the faces. You will still be warm. Bring water.

Summer is also when a more fashion-led couple's session works, if that is what you want. A real outfit, a location with architecture, and direction that is closer to editorial than to a picnic. Say so. If you want to look like yourselves on a walk, say that instead. I can do both. I do not like guessing in the parking lot.

Smoke can flatten a summer sky. If the forecast is thick, I will tell you whether to keep the day. Some smoke is a soft box the size of the province. Some is just grey. We decide with the actual sky, not with optimism.

## Autumn colour, and one red sweater

Autumn is the season I trust. The light goes warm, the valley has colour, and a couple in real clothes looks finished without a stylist. A red sweater in that light will dominate the frame, which is wonderful when one of you wants to be the colour and the other is happy in black, navy, or denim. It is less wonderful when both of you wear the same loud red and the trees join in. One strong colour between you is usually enough.

Fallen leaves are slippery. I keep poses on ground I have walked. I will not put you on a wet slope for a dip that looks like every other autumn photograph in the country. If you want to be close, we can be close on flat ground and let the colour sit behind you.

Timing matters more than the calendar week. A week of wind can take the colour off a path. I watch the trees on the routes I actually use, and I will move us to a street with architecture if the valley has already dropped its leaves. You hired pictures, not a specific branch.

## Winter, if you like the cold

Winter couples pictures are some of my favourites, and they are not for everyone. The city goes graphic. Dark coats, breath, snow in the trees, a bridge that looks stricter than it did in July. If you like that, we will make a tight, beautiful session. If you hate the cold, we will not pretend a long outdoor hour is romantic.

I keep winter shorter. Faces first, while you are still relaxed. Then the wider frames. Then we stop, even if I can imagine one more spot. The extra spot is how people start to look like they are enduring me.

Boots you can stand in. A coat that is the outfit, not a coat you peel off into a summer dress. Gloves you can lose for a few frames and find again. If you want a more formal look under a coat, we do it beside a building that blocks the wind, and we do it quickly. The [location guide](/journal/edmonton-photoshoot-locations/) covers which bridges and grounds are worth it in the cold.

Tell me if either of you has trouble with ice, long stands, or cold hands. I will change the plan. Pride is a bad reason to suffer through a session.

## What to decide before we meet

Who is in the pictures. Whether kids or a dog are part of it, and for how long. What the pictures are for: an announcement, a wall, a small set for the two of you. How you feel about being directed. Most people have never been directed, and that is fine. I will give you small jobs, not a speech.

Matching outfits are optional. Looking intentional is not. If one of you arrives in a suit and the other in gym clothes you meant to change out of, we will still shoot, and we will also have a shorter menu of frames that can survive the mismatch. A quick change in the car solves more than people think.

Public places are shared. I do not block a path, and I do not ask strangers to move. If you want a kiss, we will find a gap. If a gap does not come, we will make a portrait that is just as close without the performance. Check the City of Edmonton notes if your plan is bigger than two people on a path. I will not invent a permit in this article.

St. Albert and Fort Saskatchewan are part of my map when they suit you. We do not need downtown for the pictures to be yours.

## After the session

You get a private gallery, usually within two weeks. I would rather you see a tight edit than every frame where you blinked. If you want a faster turn for a specific date, tell me when you book, not after the shoot.

There is no price list in this note. Every session is different, so pricing is on request. Write to me through the [booking page](/book/) with the season you have in mind, the neighbourhood you live in, and whether you want easy or a little more fashion. I will reply with options.

If a gown is part of a later day, the [bridal portrait note](/journal/bridal-portraits-edmonton/) is the next read. Couples pictures and bridal portraits can both exist. They should not be crushed into the same hour unless we planned that crush on purpose.
