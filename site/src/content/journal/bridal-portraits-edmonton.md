A bridal portrait is a session in the dress, on a day that is not the wedding. That distinction matters. On the wedding day I am working around a schedule, a family, and a hundred small deadlines. On a portrait day the dress gets time. We can walk, we can wait for a cloud, and we can fix a hem without a procession behind us.

I started in weddings years ago. These days the work I want is fashion, editorial, and portraits, and bridal sits in that portrait world with a fashion edge. If you want the wedding day itself covered as a full event, say that plainly when you write. If you want portraits, say that too. They are not the same job, and I will not blur them in a quote.

You can see the tone I like in the broader [portraits, bridal, and couples](/portraits-bridal-couples/) work. This note is how I plan the bridal ones.

## A bridal portrait is not the wedding day

The gift of a separate session is calm. You already know how the dress feels. You have worn the shoes for more than the walk to the car. Hair and makeup can be done for photographs, not for a twelve-hour day. We are not stealing time from a dinner.

It is also a rehearsal for your face. A lot of people have never been directed in a gown. The first ten minutes feel odd. Then the shoulders drop, and the pictures start. I would rather that learning happen on a Tuesday than in the twenty minutes after a ceremony.

If you want both an engagement session and a bridal portrait, they can be different days and different clothes. The engagement is about the two of you in real life. The bridal portrait is about the dress and the person wearing it. Mixing them into one rushed hour makes both thinner.

## Edmonton backdrops that suit a gown

Edmonton has stone, glass, and bridges, and a gown will use all three differently. The Legislature grounds give you columns and a formal walk. A bridge gives you steel and sky. A glass atrium, when we are allowed to be there, gives you a bright ceiling and a softer kind of grandeur. I only shoot interiors we actually have permission to use. A pretty lobby is not an invitation.

Outdoors, wind is the co-director. A long train needs space and a person who knows how to move it, or it becomes a pile. Tell me if the dress has a train, a cape, or a bow that needs a wide frame. I will pick a path, not a stairwell.

Snow and a white dress can be beautiful and can also erase the hem. I look for a dark window grid, a brick step, a tree line, or a coat of shadow so the dress has an edge. If the day is a whiteout, I will say so and we will move closer to architecture.

For a map of the outdoor places I use most, read [where I like to shoot in Edmonton](/journal/edmonton-photoshoot-locations/). Before a large setup, a crowd, or any gear that claims a public space, check the City of Edmonton site. I do not publish permit rules here, because they change and I will not guess them for you.

## St. Albert, a short drive and a different scale

St. Albert is part of where I work, and it does not look like downtown. The scale is quieter. The historic grain elevators and the paths along the Sturgeon are public reference points people already know, and they photograph as a small-city landscape rather than a capital skyline. That difference is the point. If your people are in St. Albert, we do not need to drag a gown into the busiest corner of Edmonton to make the picture "count."

I will not invent a venue history I do not have. If you want a specific hall, garden, or street, tell me, and we will confirm we are allowed to photograph there. The City of St. Albert publishes its own park rules. Read those before you plan anything beyond a simple session on foot. I will read them with you if the plan is more than that.

Fort Saskatchewan is the same idea, further out. I am happy to travel in Alberta when the drive serves the picture. A long drive to a location that looks like the one ten minutes away is not a favour. I will tell you which is which.

## Seasons, and when a gown is happier

Summer light lasts, and so do mosquitoes and heat under a lined dress. We plan shade, a shorter first look at the full sun, and a longer stretch in open shade. You will still be warm. Bring water, and a robe or a coat for the walk from the car.

Autumn is kind to photographs. The light goes warm earlier, and a pale dress against colour has a fashion feeling without a stylist on set. The ground can be wet. A hem that costs a fortune should not be the thing we discover is dragging. Bring a way to lift it, or tell me and I will direct you so it stays up.

Winter bridal portraits are a choice. They look like Alberta, and they ask more of you. We keep the session tighter, we watch the wind on the veil, and we get the face while you still feel like yourself. I will not extend a winter gown session to prove endurance.

Spring is melt, mud, and sudden sun. I book a backup corner with pavement. Pretty mud is a fashion idea. Surprise mud on a hem you still need for the wedding is not.

## Who comes with you

A partner, a parent, or a friend can help with a train and can also fill the frame with nerves. I usually ask for one person who is useful, and I ask everyone else to stand where I point, which is often outside the lens. If you want them in a few frames, we do those on purpose at the end, when the dress pictures already exist.

Your hair and makeup artist is welcome. If you do not have one, I can recommend local artists. I do not do the makeup. An artist who has seen the dress in daylight will serve you better than a look designed for a dark reception hall.

If a child is with you, tell me the age and the attention span. I can make room. I cannot make a toddler wait through a fashion sequence, and I will not pretend otherwise in the plan.

## How I plan the session

We start with a chat. You tell me the dress, the feeling, and whether this is portraits only. I suggest locations and timing. We meet, I direct, and the edited pictures go to a private gallery. That is the same three steps I list on the [services](/services/) page, and bridal does not get a secret fourth step.

I need to know when the wedding is, so I know how careful the hem must be, and when you need the gallery. Usual delivery is within two weeks. If you need a tighter date, say it at the start, not the week before.

Pricing is on request. A short portrait on one corner is not the same as a fashion-led session with two locations and a gown that needs time. Send the details when you [book a shoot](/book/) and I will reply with options. I will not put a number in an article that would be wrong for your dress and your day.

If you are still choosing what the portrait should feel like, the [what to wear](/journal/what-to-wear-photoshoot/) note applies even in a gown: walk in it at home, and tell me what you cannot do in it. I will build the pictures around the truth of the dress, not around a pose you cannot hold.
